\section{Problem and motivation}
Isomap replaces ambient Euclidean distance by graph shortest-path distance
before multidimensional scaling \cite{tenenbaum2000}. A disconnected graph
does not provide finite path distances between every pair of observations.
Moreover, embedding connected components independently leaves their relative
translations and orientations undetermined.

Let $X=\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}$. Construct the radius
graph $G_r$ using Euclidean edge weights and
\[
  (i,j)\in E_r \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  i\ne j,\ \|x_i-x_j\|_2\le r .
\]
Its connected components are $C_1,\ldots,C_K$. Graph components are
\emph{candidates} for separate manifolds; sampling and inappropriate
neighborhood radii can incorrectly split or merge a true manifold.

The research question is: \emph{how much intercomponent placement accuracy
must be sacrificed to retain the geometry of independently embedded
components while guaranteeing that their projections remain separate?}
In particular, no finite intrinsic geodesic exists between distinct
disconnected generating manifolds; such cross-component targets must
instead be explicitly defined, for example as original ambient distances.
